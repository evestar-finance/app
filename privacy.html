2. What this policy covers

This policy covers Evestar Accounting Bridge (the "Application"), an internal software tool operated by Evestar Group, the personal information the Application handles, and the public web pages where this policy is published.

The Application is for internal use only. It exists for one purpose: to let authorized personnel of Evestar Group LLC work with the QuickBooks Online companies of Evestar Group and of its affiliated companies; the QuickBooks company of an affiliated company is connected only after that company has authorized Evestar Group in writing to connect it to the Application and to process its records as described in this policy. It is not distributed to anyone, it is not listed on the Intuit App Store or any other marketplace, and it is not offered as a service. It has no external users. There is no sign-up, no customer account and no user base. If you are reading this policy, you are not a user of the Application; you may be a person whose information appears inside accounting records that the Application accesses, or a reviewer evaluating the Application.

The public web pages are static. They set no cookies and run no scripts. They are served by GitHub Pages, a third-party hosting service that may record technical information about visits, such as IP addresses, under its own terms. When a QuickBooks company is connected, Intuit returns the browser to one of these pages with a one-time authorization code and the identifier of that QuickBooks company in the page address, so GitHub Pages may also record that address.

This policy does not cover:

  • QuickBooks Online itself. QuickBooks Online is operated by Intuit Inc., which is the source and the system of record for the data described below. Intuit's handling of that data is governed by Intuit's own privacy statement, published at https://www.intuit.com/privacy/.
  • Any other business process, website or system of Evestar Group.

3. What information the Application accesses

The Application connects to QuickBooks Online using OAuth 2.0 with a single scope, com.intuit.quickbooks.accounting. Through that scope it can read accounting records of 41 QuickBooks entity types: Account, Attachable, Bill, BillPayment, Budget, Class, CompanyCurrency, CompanyInfo, CreditCardPayment, CreditMemo, Customer, CustomerType, Department, Deposit, Employee, Estimate, ExchangeRate, Invoice, Item, JournalCode, JournalEntry, Payment, PaymentMethod, Preferences, Purchase, PurchaseOrder, RecurringTransaction, RefundReceipt, ReimburseCharge, SalesReceipt, TaxAgency, TaxClassification, TaxCode, TaxPayment, TaxRate, TaxService, Term, TimeActivity, Transfer, Vendor and VendorCredit. It can create or change records of 35 of them; the others can only be read.

In practice this includes:

a) Personal information about third parties contained in the accounting records, that is, about the customers, vendors, contractors and employees of Evestar Group and of its affiliated companies:

  • Name and display name
  • Company or business name
  • Email address
  • Telephone number
  • Billing address, shipping address and "ship from" address, including city, postal code and state, and in some records the latitude and longitude that Intuit derives from the address

b) Business and financial information: invoice and bill numbers, line items, descriptions, unit prices, quantities, totals, balances, tax codes and tax percentages, payments, deposits, journal entries, terms, time activities and account references.

c) Attachments uploaded to or downloaded from QuickBooks (for example, invoice PDFs or supporting documents).

d) OAuth tokens issued by Intuit that allow the Application to act on a connected QuickBooks company, plus the associated company identifier (realmId).

e) Technical logs generated locally by the Application (see sections 7 and 8).

The single accounting scope granted by Intuit permits both reading and writing. The Application can create, update, void and delete accounting records in each connected company.

4. What the Application does not collect

  • No payment card data and no bank account or routing numbers are requested. The Application does not ask for them and has no function that handles them. Fields such as BankAccountRef or CCAccountRef that appear in QuickBooks records are references to entries in the chart of accounts; they contain no account numbers.
  • No Social Security Numbers are requested. The Application does not ask for them and has no function dedicated to storing them.
  • No identity scopes. The Application requests only the accounting scope named in section 3. It does not request the OpenID, profile, email, phone or address scopes, nor the QuickBooks Payments scope, and therefore does not receive Intuit account profile information about the person who approves the connection.
  • No analytics, telemetry, advertising or tracking. The Application uses only the Python standard library and declares no external packages; it contains no analytics component, no crash or error reporting service and no usage tracking.
  • No data about the general public. The Application only sees what is already inside the QuickBooks companies connected to it.

8. Security measures: what actually exists

We describe here only controls that exist in the code or on the computer. We do not claim anything else.

In transit. Every outbound connection made by the Application's own code uses HTTPS with certificate validation and hostname verification, using the default settings of the Python runtime and the trust store of the operating system. Certificate validation is not disabled anywhere in the code. Attachment downloads additionally require HTTPS and a host under one of these domains (amazonaws.com, cloudfront.net, intuit.com, intuit.net, quickbooks.com). The check compares domain suffixes, so any host under those domains is accepted; where one of them is a shared cloud storage or content delivery domain, the check limits downloads to that provider's hosts, not to Intuit alone. There is no certificate pinning and no mutual TLS.

OAuth tokens on disk. The token file is encrypted with Windows DPAPI in user scope, which ties decryption to the specific Windows account on the specific computer: a copy of the file taken to another computer or another account cannot be decrypted. The Application also restricts the permissions of the token file to the operator's Windows account and warns if it cannot do so. On Windows, if DPAPI protection is unavailable or fails, the Application refuses to write the tokens instead of saving them unprotected. The DPAPI protection of the token file is the only encryption of stored data that this policy describes.

Application credentials. The Intuit client secret is stored without encryption in a local configuration file (.env.prod) whose file permissions are restricted to the Windows account of the operator. An administrator of that computer could still take control of the file.

Logs. The local log files are plain text and are not encrypted. They contain the personal information and the amounts described in section 3, and they may contain temporary signed addresses that Intuit issues for attachments.

Secret masking. Before a record is written to the log files, the Application masks access tokens, refresh tokens, the client secret and authorization headers, including in error records.

Record of writes. Writes to QuickBooks made through the Application are recorded locally, with timestamp, operation, entity, identifier and, by default, the data sent and the response received. These files are plain text on the local disk that the same operator can edit or delete; they are not signed, not chained and not forwarded to any external log system.

Safeguards against accidental changes. The Application has a dry-run mode that stops any write before it reaches the network. Its MCP server requires an explicit confirmation value before any of its 9 tools that create, change, void or delete QuickBooks records, upload an attachment or send a document by email acts, and returns a preview instead of executing when that value is absent. 2 other tools (qbo_attach_download, qbo_pdf) read from QuickBooks and save files on the computer; they ask for that confirmation only when a specific destination is named, and without one they use the default downloads folder of the company profile or do not write to disk. Files that the MCP server downloads or uploads are limited to designated folders of the company profile.

Access control. The Application has no user accounts, login, roles or permissions of its own. Access is controlled by the Windows session of the operator, by the protection of the token file described above and by the file permissions of the computer. The Application itself provides no multi-factor authentication, no single sign-on and no role-based access control. None of these protections helps against an unlocked computer or against malicious software running under the same Windows account.

What we do not have. No independent security certification, attestation, external security audit or third-party security testing has been obtained or performed for the Application.

Incident response. Security reports should be sent to eduard@evestar.com. Evestar Group does not currently have a written incident-response procedure and has not committed to a specific notification timeline; this is stated as it is. Where notification is required by law, Evestar Group will notify as the law requires.

Intuit, and the service providers behind the software tools that authorized personnel of Evestar Group LLC use to operate the Application. Stated precisely:

This happens only under the direct instruction of authorized personnel of Evestar Group LLC. The transmission is performed by the client software, not by the Application's own code, but it is a disclosure to a third party and this policy states it as such.
  • Backup and file-synchronization software. The Application itself makes no backups. If backup or file-synchronization software is configured on the computer described in section 7, that software may copy the local files described there to its own storage, under its own terms.
  • There are no other sub-processors, no analytics providers, no advertising networks and no data brokers built into the Application itself.
  • Evestar Group does not sell personal information.
  • Evestar Group may disclose information when required by law, for example in response to a valid subpoena, court order or tax authority request, or to its professional advisors (accountants, attorneys, auditors) bound by confidentiality.
  • Some of the processing described above may take place outside the United States: the computer described in section 7 is located in the United States, and the service providers described above may process this information in the countries where they operate.

10. How long the information is kept

Accounting records in QuickBooks Online are retained for as long as the business and legal obligations of their owner require, under the control of Intuit and subject to Intuit's own retention practices.

OAuth tokens stored locally are deleted from the computer when the connection is revoked from the tool. Access tokens are short-lived and are renewed automatically; refresh tokens are rotated by Intuit and the newest value replaces the previous one. Deleting the token file is an ordinary file deletion; it does not overwrite the contents on disk.

Local log files (logs\qbo_audit.jsonl and logs\qbo_requests.jsonl) contain the personal information and amounts described in section 3. The Application rotates each log file when it reaches 10 MB, keeping up to 5 older copies; older content beyond that limit is deleted automatically, so the history kept on the computer depends on the volume of activity. The log files are not deleted when a connection is revoked. The proposed maximum retention period for these files is five (5) years; it is proposed and not yet an adopted policy.

The local configuration file with the client secret is not deleted by revoking a connection; it has to be removed manually.

Downloaded files in downloads\, and any other folder the operator chose, are not deleted automatically by the Application.

Copies held by the client software and its provider. The local history kept by the client software described in section 9 is not deleted by the Application; it is kept and deleted according to the settings of that software. Records transmitted to the service provider described in section 9 are retained by that provider under its commercial terms; Evestar Group does not control that retention.

How to exercise them. Write to eduard@evestar.com with the subject line "Privacy request - Evestar Accounting Bridge". How we respond. Requests are handled manually: a person locates the records in QuickBooks Online and acts on them. We will respond within the time required by applicable law; for requests under the California Consumer Privacy Act, that means within 45 days of receipt, which may be extended once by up to 45 additional days where reasonably necessary, in which case we will tell you about the extension.

Limits on deletion, stated honestly. Some information cannot be deleted on request, because accounting, tax and contract records must be retained under applicable law and because invoices, payments and journal entries are financial records that must remain auditable. Where we cannot delete, we will tell you why. The local log files described in section 7 contain historical copies of records; a deletion request affecting them is handled manually.

If your information reached us through QuickBooks Online, note that Intuit operates that platform; requests about Intuit's own processing should be addressed to Intuit.

Employees and job applicants in California have additional rights under the CCPA as it applies to employment-related information.

13. How to revoke the Application's access to QuickBooks

Any of the following ends the Application's access to a QuickBooks company:

  • From QuickBooks Online, a user of that company with sufficient rights can disconnect "Evestar Accounting Bridge" from the list of connected apps in the company settings. The next attempt by the Application to renew its access fails.
  • From the tool itself, the revoke function calls Intuit's revocation endpoint and deletes the local token file of that company.
  • From the Intuit developer portal, regenerating the client secret of the application prevents the old secret from renewing any access, so every connection of the application stops working once its current access token expires.

Revoking access does not delete the local log files, the downloaded files or the local history of the client software, and it does not delete the accounting data inside QuickBooks Online.
